# H06 — Interference-flagged sessions still leave enough valid ones

**Status:** AI drafted (no human review yet) · **Last updated:** 2026-09-20 · **Framing:** `docs/PROBLEM_STATEMENTS.md` §5 step 3 · **Registry:** `docs/hypotheses/README.md`

## Statement (falsifiable)

The client's session-quality rule — "flag sessions where another appliance causes a significant overlapping change" [client §5 step 5] — keeps enough good sessions that, in a normal household, at least 3 **valid** sessions are obtainable for a typical device within a practical time budget, **and** profiles built from flag-filtered sessions outperform profiles built ignoring the flags.

Two sub-claims, each falsifiable on its own:
(a) **yield:** flagging does not discard so much that "3 valid" becomes impractical;
(b) **quality:** the flag is directionally right — filtered profiles beat unfiltered ones.

## Why it matters

- This is the client's own assumption and **nobody has ever measured it** — the only hypothesis in the registry with zero evidence of any kind.
- It is the bridge between the product's calibration UX (H01) and detection quality (H02): if flagging is too aggressive, enrollment fails; too lax, profiles inherit contamination exactly where the method is weakest (long-session devices).

## How to verify

- Contaminated-session simulation: for device d, sample K sessions as in H02, then inject contaminator episodes into a controlled fraction of session windows, drawn at realistic rates from the reviewed priors (fridge cycles every 10-40 min; ~half of target onsets begin while another target runs [`00_overview.md` §6]); once H12's measured per-dataset contamination rates exist, they replace the prior-based injection rates.
- **Operationalize the flag first** (frozen before the run): the client doc says "significant" without a number. Working candidate: any sustained step > X W or > Y% of local baseline inside the session window, X/Y fixed at freeze time.
- Measure: flag precision/recall against the injected truth; valid-session yield vs contamination rate; profile quality (anchor-relative, H02 metrics) with flag-filtered vs unfiltered sessions.
- Convert yield into product terms: expected number of calibration attempts per valid session, per device class.
- Also test **salvage**: use the clean sub-interval of a flagged session (everything up to the contaminator's onset) instead of discarding the whole session. If salvage works, busy-period calibration gets cheap — the counterweight to "prefer quiet periods".
